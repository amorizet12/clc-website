Curb Appeal That Lasts for Decades

Your driveway takes daily abuse from vehicles, weather, and temperature swings, so it lives or dies by what happens before the concrete ever gets poured. We over-excavate and properly compact the base, place reinforcement where it's needed, and cut control joints in the right spots to manage cracking before it starts.

From a simple two-car driveway to a long approach with a turnaround, we plan the layout, slope, and drainage so water moves away from your home and garage — not toward it.

  • Proper base prep and compaction
  • Reinforcement (rebar or wire mesh)
  • Control joints to manage cracking
  • Broom or exposed aggregate finish
  • Curb cuts and apron work

FAQ

Questions, Answered

How thick should a residential driveway be?

We typically pour 4 inches for standard passenger vehicles, going thicker with added reinforcement if you're regularly parking heavier vehicles like RVs or work trucks.

Will my new driveway crack?

All concrete can crack over time — our job is to control where it happens. Proper base prep, reinforcement, and correctly spaced control joints keep cracking minimal and predictable instead of random and unsightly.

How long until I can park on it?

Light foot traffic is fine after about 24 hours. We recommend waiting 7 days before parking vehicles on it, and a full 28 days before it reaches full strength.
